The best free alternatives to Grammarly

1. LanguageTool: the best all-round free choice

LanguageTool is the closest thing to “Grammarly, but free” for most people. Its free plan checks grammar, spelling and style on its website in more than 20 languages — Grammarly only checks English — and its core is open source, so you can even run your own server and keep your text on your own network. A paid Premium plan raises the limits, but the free checks cover everyday writing.

2. QuillBot: the fastest for a quick check

QuillBot’s grammar checker is free in the browser: paste your text and it highlights errors you can fix one at a time or all at once, with no account needed for a basic check. QuillBot is a paraphrasing tool first and a grammar checker second, so pick it if you rewrite sentences as often as you fix typos.

3. Hemingway Editor: free help for clearer writing

Hemingway Editor is not a grammar checker — it makes your writing clearer. Its free web app highlights long sentences, passive voice and hard words, and gives your text a readability grade. Use it after a grammar checker, not instead of one.

4. Harper: the free, private, open-source choice

Harper is free and open source, and every check runs on your own device, so your text never leaves your computer. It works in the browser, in Word and in code editors, but it only supports English and it will not rewrite whole paragraphs for you. Pick Harper if privacy matters most. Frequently asked questions

What is the best free alternative to Grammarly?

LanguageTool. It is the best all-round free choice: it checks grammar, spelling and style in more than 20 languages, its core is open source, and the free plan covers everyday writing on its website.

Is LanguageTool really free?

Yes. LanguageTool’s free plan checks your text on its website at no cost, with a limit on how much text you can check at once. A paid Premium plan raises those limits and adds extra checks, but you do not need it for basic grammar and spelling.

Is there a free grammar checker that works offline?

Yes — Harper. It is free and open source, and every check runs on your own device, so no text ever leaves your computer. It only supports English, and it checks grammar rather than rewriting your text.

Do I still need Grammarly if I use a free alternative?

Not necessarily, but do not write Grammarly off. Its free plan still checks spelling, grammar and tone for free, and in independent tests it scores close to the best free alternatives. The alternatives win on specific things: LanguageTool on languages, Harper on privacy, and Hemingway on clarity.
